How it works

Know what you're buying before you buy it.

Every project gets a plain-language scope and one price before anything is built. Here's what happens at each step, and what you actually see.

1

Understand the workflow

We look at how you run things now — usually a call, sometimes a short visit — to see where the breakdowns actually are: the calls that get missed, the spreadsheet that's really the system, the six logins nobody loves.

You seeA short list, in writing, of where time and money are actually going — before we talk about building anything.
2

Agree the scope

We write down exactly what gets built and one number for it. For a website, that often comes with a working prototype so you're deciding on something real, not a proposal document.

You seeA plain-language scope and a price to say yes or no to. Saying no costs you nothing.
3

Build & validate in stages

We build the piece with the fastest payoff first, show it working, and adjust before moving to the next piece. A website can be live in days. Custom software rolls out in stages over weeks — a typical larger build runs about six weeks, with something usable live by week two, though the real number depends on your scope.

You seeUsable pieces of the system landing as they're finished, in the order that pays back fastest — not one big reveal at the end.
4

Maintain & improve

Once it's live, we stay on it: hosting, monitoring, small adjustments, and improvements as your business changes — under a maintenance agreement set out at the start, with one person to call.

You seeThe site, the software, the data and the domain belong to you, as set out in your agreement — and one contact when something needs to change.

No packages, one number

This isn't a pricing tier. It's a scope built around your business.

There's no per-seat fee and no feature tier to grow into. You get a number for the thing you actually need, and it's yours once it's built.
